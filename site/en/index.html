What's on the belt

Six modules you turn on and off one by one in Settings. Every shortcut can be changed.

  • Menu barIcons that don't fit move to a panel below the belt.
  • Window switcherEvery open window, with search and keyboard actions.
  • ClipboardCopied text, images and screenshots, ready to paste.
  • WindowsHalves, thirds and corners with ⌃⌥ shortcuts.
  • System monitorMemory, swap and disk, with a heads-up before you run out.
  • Claude accountsSwitch Claude Code accounts and see each one's usage.

No icon lost behind the notch

On a MacBook with a notch, whatever doesn't fit in the menu bar simply disappears. Toolbelt keeps only what you want in view and tucks the rest into a panel right below the belt. Click an icon there and the app's menu opens as usual.

  • Click the belt to open the panel with your hidden icons, Claude accounts and system stats.
  • ⌥-click turns on arrange mode: hold ⌘ and drag whatever should stay hidden to the left of the ┊ divider.
  • Right-click shows the modules' quick menu.
  • Next to the belt you can show the active Claude account, the 5-hour usage, RAM, swap or free disk space, all in a single item.

Switch between windows, not apps

Hold ⌥ and press ⇥: every open window from every app shows up. Let go to switch. A quick tap jumps straight back to the previous window, and ⇧ goes backwards.

Prefer ⌘⇥? Record that shortcut and Toolbelt takes over from the macOS switcher while it's running.

  • ⌥` shows only the current app's windows.
  • With the panel open W closes the window, M minimizes it, H hides the app and Q quits it.
  • / searches by window title or app name; confirm with ⏎.
  • Window thumbnails, if you want them. They're kept in memory only, 24 at most.

Everything you copied, one shortcut away

⌥⌘V opens a panel with your latest text, images and screenshots. Pick one with the mouse, the arrow keys or the numbers 1 to 0, and it's pasted into the app you were using.

  • Screenshots saved with ⌘⇧3, 4 or 5 land in the history on their own.
  • Passwords stay out: anything copied from password managers and the apps you choose to ignore is never stored.
  • Daily archive, if you turn it on: one .md file per day with the time and source app, and images in a subfolder.
  • Your way: theme, glass, frosted or solid, width, font, corners and where the panel opens.

Memory and disk in view, with a heads-up before they run out

RAM in use, swap and free disk space show up in the menu bar or in the belt's panel. When the disk crosses its limit, swap grows too large or memory pressure rises, you get a notification, and the icon turns orange or red until things are back to normal.

  • One alert at a time: each warning shows up once and only comes back after everything is back to normal.
  • Your own limits: by default, less than 10% free disk and more than 4 GB of swap.
  • Mac report with battery, storage, memory, CPU, processes, security and network. It only runs when you open it.

Several Claude Code accounts, switched in one click

Each account keeps its own login, while conversations, memory, skills and settings stay shared. Toolbelt shows which account is active, how much of the 5-hour window each one has used and when it resets.

  • A colored badge per account, in the color you pick, in the menu bar and in the panel.
  • A terminal pinned to an account, with a shortcut for each one.
  • No extra login: usage is read from Claude Code's own files and updates when they change.

Light, and everything stays on your Mac

One process instead of six. Toolbelt reacts to system events, like switching apps, a file changing or the time of the next reset, instead of constantly scanning your Mac, and it sits at 0% CPU when you're not using it.

No account, no telemetry. What you copy, your Claude usage and your system stats never leave your computer. The only thing it looks up online is whether there's a new version.

It updates itself, when you say so

Toolbelt checks for new versions. When one is out, it tells you in the belt's panel and with a system notification. One click on Update downloads it, swaps the app and reopens it, without a trip to the browser.

The permissions it asks for, and why

Accessibility required
To paste the item you pick, move and switch windows, and open hidden menu bar icons.
Notifications
For memory and disk warnings, and to tell you a new version is out.
Screenshots folder
To add the screenshots macOS saves, usually to the Desktop, to your clipboard history.
Screen Recording optional
Only for the switcher's thumbnails, which are off by default. Previews stay in memory and are never written to disk.

Install

  1. Download Toolbelt

    The .dmg file contains the app signed and notarized by Apple, so it opens without the unidentified developer warning.

  2. Drag it to Applications

    Open the .dmg and drag Toolbelt into the Applications folder.

  3. Open it and allow Accessibility

    The first time, turn Toolbelt on in System Settings › Privacy & Security › Accessibility. The belt shows up in the menu bar.

Prefer the terminal? With Homebrew:

brew install --cask nandobrazil/tap/toolbelt

FAQ

Is Toolbelt safe?

It's signed with a Developer ID and notarized by Apple: macOS checks the signature before opening it and doesn't show the "can't be verified" warning. It runs only on your Mac, with no account and no telemetry.

Is it open source?

Yes. Toolbelt's code is on GitHub under the GPL-3.0 license: you can read it, build it and change it. Anyone who distributes a modified version has to publish its code under the same license.

I already use other apps for this. Do I need to uninstall them?

No need to uninstall anything. If you already use another app to switch windows, organize the menu bar, snap windows or keep a clipboard history, quit it and remove it from your login items: the shortcuts are the same and both would compete for the menu bar.

Does Toolbelt use the internet?

Only to check for a new version and to download it when you ask. Clipboard history, Claude usage and system stats stay on your Mac.

Can I use just some of the modules?

Yes. Each module turns on and off in Settings, and every shortcut can be changed or turned off.

How do I uninstall it?

In Settings, turn off “Open Toolbelt at login”. Then quit from the belt's panel with Quit Toolbelt and drag the app from Applications to the Trash. To also remove your settings and history, delete ~/Library/Preferences/dev.oconde.toolbelt.plist and the ~/Library/Application Support/Toolbelt folder.
